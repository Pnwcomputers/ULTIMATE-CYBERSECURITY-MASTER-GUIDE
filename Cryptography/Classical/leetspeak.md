Leet tiers, basic and advanced replacement charts, and leet grammar and slang.

---


Leetspeak ("elite speak") grew out of 1980s bulletin board systems (BBS) and early hacker, phreaker and gaming culture. It was used to signal in-group status and to slip past keyword filters on boards and chat systems. It works by replacing letters with numbers, punctuation or symbols that share a similar *shape* (or occasionally a similar *sound*).

## The Leet Tiers

Leet is not one alphabet; it is a spectrum. The further down the table, the harder it is for humans (and naive filters) to read.

| Tier | Name | What changes | `HACKER` | `PASSWORD` |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 | Plain | Nothing | `HACKER` | `PASSWORD` |
| 1 | Basic leet | Vowels + S/T → digits | `H4CK3R` | `P455W0RD` |
| 2 | Standard leet | Most shape-alike digits (B, G, L, Z too) | `H4CK3R` | `P455W0RD` |
| 3 | Structural | Letters drawn from ASCII strokes | `\|-\|/-\(\|<3\|2` | `\|*/-\55\/\/()\|2\|)` |
| 4 | Symbolic | Unicode/currency look-alikes | `♓₳©₭€®` | `₱₳§§ω()®Ð` |
| 5 | Mixed / Ultra | Random mix of tiers + case | `\|-\|4(k3Я` | `P@$5\^/0\|2Ð` |

## Advanced ("Ultra") Leet Chart

Structural leet draws each letter from multiple ASCII strokes. Symbolic leet borrows characters from currency signs, other scripts and symbol blocks.

## Leet Grammar and Slang

Leet also developed its own spelling and grammar conventions:

| Convention | Example | Meaning / origin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Deliberate typos | `teh`, `pwn` | "the", "own" (p is next to o) |
| `-zor` / `-xor` suffix | `h4xx0r`, `pwnz0r` | Agent noun, "one who hacks/owns" |
| `-age` suffix | `pwnage`, `w00tage` | Noun form of a verb |
| `z` for plural `s` | `w4r3z`, `t00lz` | Warez, tools |
| `!!!1!` / `!!11one` | `w00t!!!11` | Parody of excited, sloppy typing (Shift released early) |
| `x` for `ks`/`cks` | `sux`, `r0x` | Sucks, rocks |
| `ph` for `f` | `phreak`, `phish` | Phone phreaking; later phishing |
