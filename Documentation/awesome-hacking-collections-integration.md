## Source-specific strengths and limitations

**Awesome-Hacking:** it links to separate repositories with separate maintainers. Additional specialized indices include [ICS](https://github.com/hslatman/awesome-industrial-control-system-security), [drones](https://github.com/nicholasaleks/Awesome-Drone-Hacking), [mainframes](https://github.com/samanL33T/Awesome-Mainframe-Hacking), [Web3](https://github.com/Anugrahsr/Awesome-web3-Security), [Node.js](https://github.com/lirantal/awesome-nodejs-security) and [physical security](https://github.com/DavidProbinsky/RedTeam-Physical-Tools). These are research branches, **not** claims of PNWC operational coverage or validated real-world capability.

**h4cker:** starts from the organized [security domains](https://github.com/The-Art-of-Hacking/h4cker/tree/master/cybersecurity-domains), [AI topics](https://github.com/The-Art-of-Hacking/h4cker/tree/master/ai), [certification roadmaps](https://github.com/The-Art-of-Hacking/h4cker/tree/master/certifications), [organized tools](https://github.com/The-Art-of-Hacking/h4cker/tree/master/training-reference/organized-tools) and [build-your-own labs](https://github.com/The-Art-of-Hacking/h4cker/tree/master/build-your-own-lab). Its [Security Onion / Proxmox](https://github.com/The-Art-of-Hacking/h4cker/blob/master/build-your-own-lab/security-onion-proxmox.md) guide is useful background, but OVS on a host does not magically mirror the physical unmanaged switch. Our [sensor-visibility checklist](../Homelab/proxmox-security-monitoring-visibility.md) preserves that limitation.

**Awesome-Hacking-Resources:** has a [learning and challenge directory](https://github.com/vitalysim/Awesome-Hacking-Resources#-sharpening-your-skills), [reverse engineering study material](https://github.com/vitalysim/Awesome-Hacking-Resources#-reverse-engineering-buffer-overflow-and-exploit-development), [AI red-teaming references](https://github.com/vitalysim/Awesome-Hacking-Resources#-ai-security--ai-red-teaming) and [tool list](https://github.com/vitalysim/Awesome-Hacking-Resources/blob/master/tools.md). Treat deprecated links, Python 2 snippets and post-exploitation tooling as historical until verified. [Training playbook](../PlayBooks/security-training-lab-pathways.md) identifies practical, permitted alternatives.

## Maintenance and safety requirements

Before using any linked project: verify the downstream owner, status, license, release/version, checksums, dependencies, disclosures, and scope of authorization. Never import entire wordlists, malicious samples, unreviewed binaries, phishing templates, botnet code, live credential corpora or radio jamming procedures simply because a directory mentions them. Prefer maintained defensive standards, passive evidence, isolated fixtures and clearly documented expected outcomes.

If a source appears in two or more lists (e.g. SecLists, Suricata, Promptfoo, OSINT Framework), retain **one PNWC home guide** and cross-link it from other categories; do not duplicate implementation instructions. These links are **documentation integration**, not tested installations.
